Account & Data Deletion Request

Google Play Data Safety Disclosure & Complete Household Data Deletion

1. Our Deletion Commitment

GlassSpend is built with privacy-first data autonomy. You have the absolute right to delete any individual bill, purchase receipt, service watch, or your complete account at any time. When you request data deletion, all associated records are permanently erased without hidden retention.

2. How to Delete Data Within the Android Application

You can execute an immediate, unrecoverable local wipe directly inside the GlassSpend Android app:

  1. Open the GlassSpend app on your Android device.
  2. Tap the Settings icon in the navigation bar.
  3. Scroll down to the Data & Privacy section.
  4. Tap Wipe Local Data & Delete Account.
  5. Review the confirmation dialog and tap Confirm Deletion.

Immediate Effect: Your local SQLite database is dropped and recreated, all active alarms and reminders are cancelled via Android AlarmManager, and all local temporary files are overwritten and deleted.

3. Web Deletion Request Form (Without App Access)

If you no longer have access to the mobile application or have uninstalled it, you can request full account deletion by emailing our automated privacy handler:

Send an email to: delete-account@rigourconsulting.com.au
Subject: GlassSpend Account Deletion Request
Include: The email address associated with your GlassSpend account or Google Play receipt ID.
Processing Time: Requests are processed within 48 hours, and a confirmation receipt is sent.

4. Data Retention Disclosures

  • Raw Bill Images: Raw bill images and documents are processed locally on-device and never stored on remote servers. Temporary local capture files are deleted immediately upon candidate confirmation.
  • Billing & Subscription Records: Google Play subscription purchase tokens and payment receipts are retained solely as required by Australian tax and accounting laws (Australian Taxation Office 5-year statutory requirement for financial records).
  • Local Cache: All temporary capture files are overwritten with random bytes prior to file deletion.
